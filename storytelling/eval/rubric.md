# Evaluation Rubric

Six criteria for judging a film pitch. Each criterion gets a one-sentence verdict: **pass**, **weak**, or **fail** with a reason.

---

## 1. Specificity

Does the pitch commit to a specific subject and central image, or does it hedge with vague gestures?

**Pass**: Names a concrete subject (a particular animal, object, process) and describes a specific visual moment.  
**Weak**: Subject is named but the central image is generic ("a lone figure in darkness").  
**Fail**: The pitch could describe any film about this general topic — no specific commitment to anything.

---

## 2. Emotional Turn

Does the film move? Is there a before and after — a shift in the emotional register from start to finish?

**Pass**: A clear emotional arc exists. The audience feels differently at the end than at the start.  
**Weak**: The tone is consistent but flat — one feeling, held for the duration, with no turn.  
**Fail**: No emotional movement detectable. The pitch is a description of imagery with no dramatic shape.

---

## 3. Earned Ending

Does the ending feel inevitable given what came before, rather than arbitrary or imposed?

**Pass**: The final image or beat grows directly from the logic of the film's structure.  
**Weak**: The ending is thematically appropriate but not causally connected to the preceding scenes.  
**Fail**: The ending could be swapped for a different ending with no loss to the film's logic.

---

## 4. Showing vs. Explaining

Does the pitch tell us what to feel, or does it describe images and let the feeling arise?

**Pass**: The pitch describes concrete images and actions. Emotion is implied, not stated.  
**Weak**: Occasional emotional labeling ("this is haunting", "the viewer feels awe") alongside imagery.  
**Fail**: The pitch primarily explains what the film is about rather than showing what we see.

---

## 5. Visual Rhyme

Is there a recurring visual motif or structural echo that ties the film together?

**Pass**: A specific image, shape, or gesture recurs with variation across the film.  
**Weak**: Tonal consistency but no specific visual element that reappears.  
**Fail**: The scenes feel like separate descriptions with no connective visual tissue.

---

## 6. Genericness Check (most important)

Would this pitch still work if you swapped the source science for a different paper with a similar topic? If yes, the pitch is not actually about this paper — it's a genre exercise.

**Pass**: The pitch is undeniably about this specific phenomenon. Remove the science and the film collapses.  
**Weak**: The science motivates the mood but the specific details could be exchanged for similar science.  
**Fail**: The pitch would survive a paper swap. It's a generic short film about [awe / loss / scale / time] that uses the paper as decoration.

---

## Overall Verdict

- **PASS**: 5-6 criteria pass, genericness check passes.
- **REVISE**: 3-4 criteria pass, or genericness check is weak. Prose-level problems. Rewrite the pitch.
- **RECONSIDER**: Genericness check fails, or fewer than 3 criteria pass. The concept itself is the problem. Return to Stage 2.
